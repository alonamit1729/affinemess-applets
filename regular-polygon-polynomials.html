A polynomial of degree n has at most n−1 local extrema. When they sit exactly at the corners of a regular (n−1)-gon, the derivative is forced to be a rescaled Chebyshev polynomial, P′(x) = c·Tn−1(x/R), and that polynomial is unique up to translation and reflection. Pick the degree, and choose a polygon with unit sides or one inscribed in the unit circle; the formulas are exact.

Regular polygon polynomials

A problem: a real polynomial P of degree n ≥ 4 has n−1 local extrema, and they are the vertices of a regular (n−1)-gon with unit side. Find the leading coefficient of P. It turns out such a polynomial exists in every degree, it is essentially unique, and it is a Chebyshev polynomial in disguise.

The answer

Write k = n−1 and let R be the circumradius of the polygon. Up to translation and the reflection y ↦ −y, the only such polynomial is

P′(x) = n(n−2)/(n−1) · T_{n−1}(x/R)

For unit sides R = 1/(2 sin(π/(n−1))), so the leading coefficient is

a = ±(n−2)/(n−1) · 2^(2n−3) · sin^(n−1)(π/(n−1))

That is 8√3 for quartics, 24 for quintics, and it eventually decays super-exponentially. On the unit circle (R = 1) it is simply (n−2)2^(n−2)/(n−1).

Why

Translate and scale (both axes together) so the polygon is centred at the origin with circumradius 1. Its vertices are (cos θ_j, sin θ_j) with θ_j = φ + 2πj/k.

  1. The derivative. The roots of P′ are the x-coordinates cos θ_j, which are distinct because they lie on a graph. Since T_k(cos θ_j) = cos kθ_j = cos kφ, they are all k roots of T_k(x) − cos kφ. Hence P′ = c(T_k − cos kφ).
  2. Integrate on the circle. By the chain rule, d/dθ P(cos θ) = −c sin θ (cos kθ − cos kφ), and product-to-sum gives
  3. Evaluate at the vertices. Since kθ_j ≡ kφ, cos(k±1)θ_j = cos(kφ ± θ_j), so
  4. Match. We need P(cos θ_j) = sin θ_j. For k ≥ 3 the vectors (1), (cos θ_j), (sin θ_j) in ℝ^k are nonzero and mutually orthogonal, so α = β = 0 and γ = 1. That forces cos kφ = 0, i.e. P′ = cT_k, with c = ∓(k²−1)/k.

Reading the steps backwards gives the converse.

A bonus

Step 3 holds for every φ: if P′ ∝ T_k − t with |t| < 1, the extrema always form an affinely regular polygon, the image of a regular one under a vertical shear and stretch. The Chebyshev case t = 0 is exactly the one with no shear.

Things to notice

The x-coordinates of the vertices are the Chebyshev nodes, the shadows of equally spaced points on the circle (the dotted lines). The orientation is forced: for odd k the polygon has a vertical mirror axis, for even k it is tilted by π/(2k), so no two vertices are stacked vertically. For n = 7 the hexagon's side equals its circumradius, so the two settings give the same polynomial.
