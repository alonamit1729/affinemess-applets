Brudno–Kaplansky, 1970 and 1974

In 1970 Brudno reduced a special family of solutions to the quartic z² = x⁴ + 38x²y² + y⁴ and generated new solutions with two classical recursions. In 1974 Brudno and Kaplansky put the same family on the cubic E₁ and found that all of it is the cyclic group generated by one point P.

1970 · the quartic z² = x⁴ + 38x² + 1 (y = 1)

x ↦ (x⁴−y⁴, 2xyz) is multiplication by 2 on the quartic and x ↦ (x(4y⁴z²−g²), y(4x⁴z²−g²)), g = x⁴−y⁴, is multiplication by −3. Each point gives a solution through A = 6xy(x²−y²), B = −(x⁴−2xyz−y⁴), C = x⁴+2xyz−y⁴ and D = z(x²+y²), E = 2xyz, F = (x²−y²)(x²+y²).

1974 · the cubic E₁: W² = (2T+1)(10T²+2T+1)

On the quartic, Lebesgue's recursion is the doubling map [2] and Desboves's is [−3]; the seed (1, 2, 13) corresponds to 2P, so from it the 1970 paper could only reach ±2·2a·3b·P — the solutions 2P, 4P, 6P, 8P, … — and 3P appeared there only through the irrational parameter 17 ± 7√5. The odd multiples, 5P in particular, were out of reach until the 1974 cubic made the whole cyclic group ⟨P⟩ ⊕ ⟨R⟩ visible.

What is being drawn

The surface V

Write Q(s,t) = s² + st − t². Bremner's surface V ⊂ P⁵, with coordinates (x, y, z, u, v, w), is cut out by the three quadrics

Q(x,u) = Q(w,z),   Q(y,v) = Q(u,x),   Q(z,w) = Q(v,y).

It is a K3 surface: a smooth complete intersection of three quadrics in P⁵.

Why its points solve the equation

The cubing identity

Q(s,t)³ − Q(t,s)³ = 2 (s⁶ − t⁶)

turns the three quadric equations, after cubing, into x⁶ − u⁶ + y⁶ − v⁶ + z⁶ − w⁶ = 0; adding them as they stand gives x² − u² + y² − v² + z² − w² = 0 (the st terms cancel in pairs). So every rational point of V is a point of M₆ ∩ M₂: an equal sum of sixth powers that is also an equal sum of squares. Panel 1 shows this literally: the two triples are two points of the curve where the superellipsoid meets the sphere.

The Brudno pencil

Following Brudno–Kaplansky's substitution, put

y = a + (λ−1)b,   z = (λ−1)a − b,   v = −(λ−1)a − λb,   w = λa − (λ−1)b.

For each λ this is a P³ with coordinates (x : u : a : b), and V ∩ P³ is the curve

Eλ:   x² − u² = (λ²−1)(a² + b²),   xu = λa² − (3λ²−5λ+3)ab − λb²,

an intersection of two quadrics in P³, i.e. an elliptic curve. The fibres Eλ sweep out V: this is an elliptic fibration π : V → P¹. Bremner's Weierstrass model is

y′² = x′³ − 2(5PQ − 2B²) x′² + 25P²Q² x′,   B = λ²−1, P = 2λ²−2λ+1, Q = λ²−2λ+2,

with torsion point T = (5PQ, 10BPQ) of order 4 (2T = (0,0)) and Mordell–Weil generators

g₁ = (5P², 15BP²),   g₂ = (5Q(2λ−1)², 10Q(λ−1)(2λ−1)(3λ²−λ+1)),   g₃ = (5P(λ−2)², 10P(λ−1)(λ−2)(λ²−λ+3)).

The singular fibres are I₂ at λ = ±1, I₄ at the complex roots of P and of Q, and I₁ at the four complex roots of 9λ⁴ − 30λ³ + 47λ² − 30λ + 9. Over the reals every fibre is connected, which is why Panel 2 always shows a single closed loop. In the picture x′ is rescaled by 5PQ and then sent to x′/(1+x′), so that the whole real locus, including the point at infinity on the right edge, fits in a fixed frame.

Sections, and Brudno's quartic

A section is a rational curve S ⊂ V meeting every fibre once; it is the same thing as a point of E(Q(λ)) = ⟨g₁, g₂, g₃⟩ ⊕ ⟨T⟩, written

S = n₁g₁ + n₂g₂ + n₃g₃ + kT,   ni ∈ Z, k ∈ Z/4.

Plugging λ = m/n into the coordinates of S and clearing denominators gives six binary forms in (m, n): a polynomial identity x⁶+y⁶+z⁶ = u⁶+v⁶+w⁶. Brudno's 1976 quartic is exactly the section (−1, 0, 1, 0) (up to the symmetries of V); Bremner's curves (ii), (iii), (iv), Choudhry's octic and Delorme's degree-9 curve are sections too, and the four remaining orbits of degree ≤ 12 (degrees 7, 10, 11, 11) are new. The eight lines Γi and eight of the conics are the sections of degree 1 and 2.

The degree formula

The degree of the section curve (as a curve in P⁵, the degree of the six forms) depends only on the lattice part:

deg S = 2n₁² + 4n₂² + 4n₃² − 2n₁n₂ − 2n₁n₃ + 2n₂n₃ + 2n₁ − 3n₂ − 3n₃ + 1 = 2⟨S − γ/4, S − γ/4⟩,   γ = −g₁ + g₂ + g₃,

where ⟨ , ⟩ is the height pairing with Gram matrix [[1, −½, −½], [−½, 2, ½], [−½, ½, 2]] on (g₁, g₂, g₃). There is no torsion correction: S, S+T, S+2T, S+3T are symmetric images of one another. Since the quadratic form is positive definite there are finitely many sections of each degree; those of degree ≤ 12 are the 144 drawn in Panel 2 (36 lattice classes × 4 torsion translates, 12 orbits under the symmetry group), and there is none of degree 3 or 12.

Brudno–Kaplansky

Brudno's 1970 ansatz v = y − z, w = y + z is the case (p, q) = (0, 1) of the substitution above, i.e. the fibre λ = 0 (equivalently λ = ∞, 2/3, 3/2 under the Klein four-group of symmetries that act on λ). On it the generators specialise with a rank-2 kernel, and the surviving generator g₁ is the Brudno–Kaplansky point P of E₁: W² = (2T+1)(10T²+2T+1), which is the curve 480f4. The quartic z² = x⁴ + 38x²y² + y⁴ of 1970 is a different model, 2-isogenous (its Jacobian is 480f1), and its rational points correspond to the even multiples of P.
