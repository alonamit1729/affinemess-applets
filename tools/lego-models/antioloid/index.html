Brick Anti-oloid

6,906 bricks (2×4 and 2×2) · 67 layers

Before you start

Take the two circles of the oloid, equal circles in perpendicular planes with each passing through the other's centre, and join them by a band of straight segments: that band is the anti-oloid. The two circles are linked once, like two links of a chain, so the band is an annulus with a full twist (a Hopf band, two of which, plumbed together, make the trefoil's surface). It has two sides, so two colours: orange and azure. Its two edges are the circles: the white one stands upright, the dark blue one lies level through its centre.

The model goes up one layer per step, from the floor. Each page shows the model after the step and a top-down map of the one layer you're working on.

Set up

  1. Lay the baseplates edge to edge, 3 across and 2 deep (144 × 96 studs). The map's column and row numbers count studs from the far-left corner as seen from the front, and the darker lines on the map, every 48 studs, are the seams between baseplates.
  2. Keep the model on the baseplates until it's finished. It stands on the bottom of the upright circle and on two grey pillars under the level circle; these are separate pieces until step 35, so the baseplates keep everything lined up.
  3. It's heavy, about 16 kg. Once finished, move it on its baseplates, lifting from underneath.

Reading a step

  • Each step builds one layer. In the picture, new bricks have bold outlines and full colour; earlier layers are faded.
  • Some steps continue with short parts (12·2, 12·3…). Each part works on one layer only. On the map, a yellow outline means place on top; a blue outline means press onto the underside of the bricks above. Parts with blue outlines also show a view from underneath.
  • Faint outlines on the map show the neighbouring layer, for lining things up. Tap any brick to see its exact columns and rows.
