Brick Borromean Rings

6,499 bricks (2×4 and 2×2) · 58 layers

Before you start

The Borromean rings are three rings, no two of them linked, that still can't be pulled apart: take any one away and the other two come loose. Here they are three ellipses in mutually perpendicular planes, and the model is a single surface whose edge is all three rings at once, a Seifert surface for the link. It is a torus with three holes (genus one), the least possible for these rings. It has two sides, so two colours: orange and azure. The rings are black, white and grey.

The model goes up one layer per step, from the floor. Each page shows the model after the step and a top-down map of the one layer you're working on.

Set up

  1. Lay the baseplates edge to edge, 2 across and 2 deep (96 × 96 studs). The map's column and row numbers count studs from the far-left corner as seen from the front.
  2. Keep the model on the baseplates until it's finished. It stands on the bottom of the surface and on three short grey pillars; the pieces join up by step 5, and the baseplates keep everything lined up until then.
  3. It's heavy, about 14 kg. Once finished, move it on its baseplates, lifting from underneath.

Reading a step

  • Each step builds one layer. In the picture, new bricks have bold outlines and full colour; earlier layers are faded.
  • Some steps continue with short parts (12·2, 12·3…). Each part works on one layer only. On the map, a yellow outline means place on top; a blue outline means press onto the underside of the bricks above. Parts with blue outlines also show a view from underneath.
  • Faint outlines on the map show the neighbouring layer, for lining things up. Tap any brick to see its exact columns and rows.
