Brick Boy's Surface

7,034 bricks (2×4 and 2×2) · 35 layers

Before you start

Boy's surface is the real projective plane immersed in space: it passes through itself along a curve but has no pinch points. This is the Bryant–Kusner version, the least bent one, with a disc cut out of the top so you can see inside. What's left is a Möbius band. Its single edge is the white rim, and the orange bricks trace the double curve, where the surface crosses itself; its three loops meet at the triple point in the middle. The surface has only one side, so it gets one colour: azure.

The model goes up one layer per step, from the floor. Each page shows the model after the step and a top-down map of the one layer you're working on.

Set up

  1. Lay the baseplates edge to edge, 2 across and 2 deep (96 × 96 studs). The map's column and row numbers count studs from the far-left corner as seen from the front.
  2. Keep the model on the baseplates until it's finished. It stands on the bottoms of its three lobes, which are separate pieces until step 7, so the baseplates keep everything lined up. No pillars are needed: once joined, it stands on its own.
  3. It's heavy, about 16 kg. Once finished, move it on its baseplates, lifting from underneath.

Reading a step

  • Each step builds one layer. In the picture, new bricks have bold outlines and full colour; earlier layers are faded.
  • Some steps continue with short parts (12·2, 12·3…). Each part works on one layer only. On the map, a yellow outline means place on top; a blue outline means press onto the underside of the bricks above. Parts with blue outlines also show a view from underneath.
  • Faint outlines on the map show the neighbouring layer, for lining things up. Tap any brick to see its exact columns and rows.
