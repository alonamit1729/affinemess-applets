# F(6) and F(5) by the mechanical method of FINITENESS.md — a consistency check

F(d): the set of d-distance sets with g(d) points is finite up to similarity. Known from the classifications for
d ≤ 6; here it is re-derived for d = 6 (n = 13) and d = 5 (n = 12) by the method of FINITENESS.md §1–2
(Wei's core lemma + pinning lemma + certified zero-dimensionality of the candidate patterns of the smaller cores),
with all pattern lists taken from the regenerated Szöllősi–Östergård levels `gen/levels/L7..L10.json`.

Summary: **both F(6) and F(5) come out**, but *not* along every row of the diameter-graph table as first
written: three candidate patterns — one 9-point and two 8-point patterns with five distances — are
**positive-dimensional** (genuine real one-parameter families, identified in §4), so the rows that reduce to 9- or
8-point ≤5-distance cores do not close by core rigidity alone. They close by a uniform replacement (any 10-point
subset is rigid), which in fact makes the diameter-graph analysis unnecessary at d = 5 and d = 6 (§3).
A side result: the "≥ 6 points" threshold stated in the pinning lemma of FINITENESS.md §1 is too optimistic as
written; the correct threshold is |A| > 2(d − k(A)) (§1 below). It does not affect F(7), whose rigid subsets
have ≥ 10 points.

## 1. The pinning lemma, corrected threshold

As in FINITENESS.md §1: A ⊂ X rigid along the arc; a class is *fixed* if it occurs inside A, *free* otherwise. A point
x ∉ A is unpinned only if at most one point of A is at a fixed-class distance from x, and then no free class holds a
pair of A-points (circle ∩ line) — so all of A but at most one point lies in free classes as singletons, |A| ≤ 1 + free;
or no point of A is at a fixed-class distance, each free class holds ≤ 2 points of A (three would make x a
circumcentre), |A| ≤ 2·free. Hence

> x is pinned whenever |A| > 2·(d − k(A)), where k(A) ≥ k_min(|A|) is the number of classes inside A.

With k_min(6) = k_min(7) = 3, k_min(8) = k_min(9) = 4, k_min(10..12) = 5 (from g(2) = 5, g(3) = 7, g(4) = 9,
g(5) = 12) this gives: a rigid subset pins the family when |A| ≥ 6 for d = 5, |A| ≥ 7 for d = 6, |A| ≥ 8 for d = 7.
(FINITENESS.md §1 says "|A| ≥ 6 … free ≤ 4" for d = 7, which would need |A| > 8; the rows of its §2 table use
|A| ≥ 10 or |X_D| ≥ 13, so the F(7) argument is unaffected.) Pinned points are added to A one at a time; the
procedure stops only when A = X.

Below, every core has ≥ 7 points, so pinning is never the issue; the only question is rigidity of the core.

## 3. The uniform replacement, and the conclusions

Any 10-point subset B of a 13-point 6-distance set has ≤ 6 distances, so its pattern is one of the 228 of L10, all
zero-dimensional: B is rigid, k(B) ≥ 5, free ≤ 1 < |B|/2, and B pins X. Likewise any 10-point subset of a 12-point
5-distance set has pattern among the 19 of L10 with ≤ 5 colours, all zero-dimensional. Hence

> **F(6)** ⇐ the 228 order-10 patterns are zero-dimensional — holds (`pat_rigid_10d6.json`: 228 zerodim).
> **F(5)** ⇐ the 19 order-10 ≤5-colour patterns are zero-dimensional — holds (`pat_rigid_10d5.json`: 19 zerodim).
> (**F(4)**, n = 9, is the statement that the 4 nine-point ≤4-colour patterns are zero-dimensional: `pat_rigid_9d4.json`.)

This also repairs every open row of the d = 6 table (a 9- or 8-point core plus any other points of X is a
10-point subset). The diameter-graph analysis is therefore only needed when the n-point sets of the next level down
are *not* known: for d = 7 the 11-point ≤7-distance patterns are unknown (the orderly generation was run with ≤ 6
colours), which is why F(7) needs the core lemma to drop the diameter colour.

The mod-p screen (msolve, `modp` field) and the ℚ certificate (Singular, `dimQ`) agreed on every pattern.
Every positive-dimensional pattern was also checked with random hyperplane sections (`posdim_real.py`,
`posdim_real.json`): each of 6 random sections has 4–8 real points with distinct nonzero values and pairwise
different shapes, and the Jacobian of the distance equations at these real points has corank exactly 1 — so the
three are smooth real one-parameter families, not complex-only curves.

**The three families are one family.** `L9#124` has colour classes of sizes 6, 6, 6, 9, 9; the three 6-classes are
three equilateral triangles with a common centre, and the closure of the stratum (Singular, `eliminate` of t)
is the curve 2s₁ = s₄ + 1, 2(s₂ + s₃) = s₄ + 1, 12s₃² − 6s₃s₄ + s₄² − 6s₃ − 2s₄ + 1 = 0 in the squared values
(s₀ = 1 the side of one triangle, s₁, s₄ the other two sides, s₂, s₃ the cross distances). It is the Minkowski sum

> X_h = T + h·R₉₀(T), T an equilateral triangle of side 1, h > 0,

(checked by pattern isomorphism, `catalog_match.py` conventions): nine points t + t', three concentric
equilateral triangles of squared sides 1 + h² + √3h, 1 + h², 1 + h² − √3h, cross distances 1 and h — five distances
for generic h. h ↔ 1/h is the mirror image. Special members: h = 1 is Erdős–Fishburn's "three concentric
equilateral triangles" nine-point four-distance set (cross classes merge, s₂ = s₃; distances √3−1, √2, 2, √3+1 up
to scale) and h = √3 (h·R₉₀T is then a lattice vector) is the triangular-lattice piece with rows 1, 3, 3, 2 — so two of
the four maximum 4-distance sets lie on one family of 5-distance sets; h → 0, ∞ are collisions. The 8-point
families are its one-point deletions: deleting a vertex of the middle triangle (side² 1 + h²) gives `L8#1081`,
deleting a vertex of the largest or the smallest triangle gives `L8#398` (the two are swapped by h ↔ 1/h).
None of the three is in the applet's (8,5)/(9,5) catalogs, which list only rigid sets (9 and 6 items).

## 5. Cross-checks against what is known

* **(9,4)**: L9 has exactly 4 four-colour patterns, all zero-dimensional and all real; the applet's (9,4) catalog
  (regular 9-gon; hexagon + centre + two adjacent outer points; lattice rows 1,3,3,2; Erdős–Fishburn's three
  triangles) realises exactly these 4 patterns, one each (`catalog_match.json`). This is Erdős–Fishburn's
  classification g(4) = 9 with four extremal sets.
* **(12,5)**: g(5) = 12 (Erdős–Fishburn 1996); the applet's (12,5) catalog has the single set "triangular-lattice
  piece, rows 3, 4, 3, 2" and its (11,5) catalog is R₁₁ plus three one-point deletions of it. The levels here stop at
  order 10, so the 12-point classification itself is not re-derived; what is re-derived is the finiteness, from
  the 19 order-10 ≤5-colour patterns (all real, all rigid; 8 of them in the applet's (10,5) catalog).
* **(13,6)**: g(6) = 13 (Wei 2012); Szöllősi–Östergård's Table 9 has 3 candidates at order 13 and the applet's (13,6)
  catalog has three sets (R₁₃, R₁₂ + centre, lattice hexagon + centre + six of the next ring). Finiteness here comes
  from the 228 order-10 patterns, which contain all 225 patterns of the known 10-point ≤6-distance sets plus the
  three R₁₂+R₁₂ sets.
* **(7,4)**: the 34 four-colour patterns are all real and rigid; the applet's (7,4) catalog (42 rigid items) realises
  all 34 (seven patterns twice or three times: `L7#880` ×3; `L7#641`, `#2458`, `#1600`, `#17`, `#2333`, `#3701` ×2) —
  the catalog's exact certification that was pending is now `pat_rigid_7d4.json`. The two three-colour patterns
  are the two 7-point 3-distance sets.
* **(8,4)**: 14 patterns, all real and rigid; the catalog's 15 items realise all 14 (`L8#494` twice). I know of no
  published count to compare with.
* **(9, ≤5)**: 57 candidate patterns, every one real-realizable (4 with four distances, 53 with five; 52 rigid +
  the family X_h). So "57" is the number of patterns of 9-point planar sets with at most five distances
  (the applet's (9,5) catalog has 6 items covering 5 patterns). No published count known to me; Szöllősi–Östergård
  only publish the ≤6-colour totals (635 at order 9).
* **(8, ≤5)**: 157 candidate patterns (14 + 143), of which **155 are patterns of real sets** (14 four-colour; 141 five-colour
  = 139 rigid + the two deletion families of X_h) and 2 (`L8#944`, `L8#988`, five colours) are complex-only: msolve real root
  isolation finds no real solution of the (zero-dimensional) coordinate system with distinct nonzero values (re-checked at a
  different precision; `L8#944` does have one real solution of the *Gram-minor* system, but with squared values
  1, 0.8, −0.2, 0.2, −0.8 and an indefinite Gram matrix — a realization in the Minkowski plane, not a Euclidean one, which is
  why the coordinate formulation is the right real test). These are the
  only complex-only candidates in the ≤5-colour lists at orders 7–10 (there are none at 7, 9, 10). They are consistent with
  hereditary generation: the 57 order-9 candidates are all real, so no real 9-point set contains them. The applet's (8,5)
  catalog (9 rigid items, 8 patterns) covers 8 of the 139 rigid real patterns.

## 6. What did not work / caveats

* The strict core-lemma route fails on the rows that need 9-point or 8-point ≤5-distance cores (d = 6: m = 7 with
  C₇, m = 8, m = 9) and on the row "X_D rigid" for d = 5, m = 9, because of the family X_h and its deletions.
  Those rows are closed only through the 10-point lists (§3). For d = 7 the analogous danger would be a
  positive-dimensional 10- or 11-point ≤6-distance pattern; there is none (FINITENESS.md §3b).
* `pat_rigid_10.json` (224 entries) was produced from an earlier `patterns_10.json`; the current file has 225
  entries with four names (`pentaligned#0, #1, #4`, `pentoffset#11`) absent from the certificate and three
  (`cat10,6#7, #11, #12`) present only there. `pat_rigid_10d6.json` supersedes it: all 228 patterns of L10
  certified directly from the level file, no name matching involved.
* Nothing here re-derives g(5) = 12 or g(6) = 13 themselves; the levels end at order 10.
* On the coordinator's heads-up (8-point and 9-point flexible 5-distance configurations found numerically inside
  R₁₂ + R₁₂): the certified lists say there is *exactly one* flexible 9-point ≤5-distance pattern and *exactly two* 8-point
  ones, all three on the family X_h = T + h·R₉₀T of §4; any 9-point 5-distance set with local dimension ≥ 1 must be a
  member of X_h (the other 56 patterns are zero-dimensional), and any flexible 8-point one a one-point deletion of a
  member. The rows at risk for F(6) are not only m = 9 with C₉ (8-point core) but also m = 7 with C₇, m = 8 and m = 9
  without C₉ (9-point cores); all are closed by "core + any further point = a rigid 10-point subset" (§3). For F(5) the
  m = 8 row (8-point ≤4-distance cores, 14 patterns, all rigid and real; the catalog's 15 sets realise them, `L8#494` twice)
  is fine.
